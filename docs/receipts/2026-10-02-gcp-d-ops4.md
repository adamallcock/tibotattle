## OPS-4: the probes

Adapted from `items-v4/OPS-4.json` to the append-only line. There is no
deletion ledger, tombstone, restore replay or erasure-job state, so the closed
names carry none, and a check refuses any such name and scans the sources for
the tokens. The Worker-era lane heartbeats and the graph-day lane do not exist
on the fast path (one refresh job over one journal), so the lane signals become
the signals that exist: the last complete `analytics_v2_runs` row, the lag from
`analytics_v2_journal_cursor` to the journal head, the pending journal rows
counted up to 10001 (`capped` set exactly at that bound, proven at 10000, 10001
and 10002 pending), and the age of the oldest undelivered row.

- **Evidence rules.** Missing data is `unavailable` with a closed reason and
  carries no value; `buildOpsProbeLine` refuses NaN, Infinity, negatives,
  strings, booleans and bigints, an unavailable line with a value, an ok line
  with a reason, and every key outside the closed set. A database error becomes
  a closed reason and its two-character SQLSTATE class; its message, detail,
  hint and query are never read (a sentinel placed in every field never
  reaches a line). Per-session signals are split by a closed application class,
  and no application name is ever printed.
- **Read only.** Every read is its own `BEGIN READ ONLY` transaction with the
  two bounds set. The spec runs each probe `SELECT` through a wrapper that asks
  the real session `SHOW transaction_read_only`, `statement_timeout`,
  `lock_timeout` and `transaction_isolation` first, and a before-and-after
  fingerprint shows no relation or row changed. A lock held on a source
  relation gave `LOCK_TIMEOUT:55` (that test, with its 500 ms bound, took 513 ms),
  and a view that sleeps 3 s gave `STATEMENT_TIMEOUT:57`.
- **Journal reads are primary-key walks.** `EXPLAIN` of the four journal
  statements on a 40,000-row, two-source journal shows the
  `storage_ingestion_changes_pkey` index and no scan, and the bounded count
  returns no more than the cap from any node.
- **The v1.2 manifest read is an index walk once the staged index is applied.**
  See "Review follow-up" below; before that, `telemetry_v12_day_manifests` had no
  index on `ready_at` or `state`.
- **Session signals depend on a role decision.** `pg_stat_activity` hides other
  roles' sessions from a role outside `pg_read_all_stats`. The probe checks
  `pg_has_role` first: without membership the three session signals are
  `PRIVILEGE_MISSING`, because a count over the visible few would read healthy
  while hiding the sessions that matter. The spec proves both sides on a real
  role (unavailable, then exact after the grant, then a revoked table giving
  `PRIVILEGE_MISSING:42`). Granting the membership to the runtime role would
  also let the origin's credentials read other sessions' query text, which the
  probe never selects, so nothing here grants it (owner, below).
- **The log-redaction marker.** A read-only transaction refuses `CREATE`
  (SQLSTATE 25006, shown on PostgreSQL 17.10), so `--probe=log-redaction` is the
  one read-write path: a TEMP table, the same random marker inserted twice, a
  real 23505, a rollback. The spec shows the table and every relation named
  `ops_probe_log_redaction` gone, the session idle and usable, and the line
  carrying only the marker. A second line reports whether the five logging
  settings OPS-2 renders still hold (0 on the local cluster, 1 once set). The
  proof itself (search the Cloud SQL logs for the marker) is a manual step in
  `gcp-ops-probes.md` and has not run.
- **Backup audit.** A fetch failure of any kind (error, timeout, status, body,
  paging past 20 pages, no credential) is `unavailable`, never ok and never a
  breach. A 366-day-old restorable copy is level 2; a copy only 40 hours old as
  the newest automated backup is level 1; point-in-time recovery off reads 0.
  Response bodies, backup descriptions, ids and the token never reach a line.
- **Deadline and exit codes.** A group past the 100 s deadline reports
  `DEADLINE_EXCEEDED` signal by signal. Exit 1 only for a configuration or pool
  failure (one stderr line, no signal lines), 2 for usage.

`OPS_PROBE_JOBS` records the item's contract (runtime probe `*/5 * * * *`, 120 s,
runtime account, one primary connection; backup audit `17 * * * *`, an
`opsBackupAudit` account with `cloudsql.instances.get` and
`cloudsql.backupRuns.list`, no connection). The manifest check pins it against
the estate: the posture settings equal `CLOUD_SQL_LOGGING_FLAGS`, both
schedules pass the manifest's cron grammar, and the runtime probe's connection
fits the budget's headroom.

## OPS-2: the maintenance job and its trigger

C-MAINT's job contract, implemented as written in
`docs/receipts/2026-10-02-gcp-c-maint.md`:

- **The trigger** is created paused at `* * * * *`, committed `PAUSED`, and
  resumed only explicitly. The validator pins the cadence to the job's own
  constant: any other schedule, and `null`, is
  `SCHEDULER_CADENCE_MISMATCH:maintenance` (a slower trigger leaves `/api/ready`
  not_ready). Apply creates it, pauses it in the same apply, then binds
  `roles/run.jobsExecutor`, and never resumes it; the plan invariant, a failed
  pause (the next plan pauses it and no grant was made meanwhile), drift,
  a running trigger and an unknown live state are each tested.
- **The job** runs `dist/postgres-maintenance-job.mjs --profile=maintenance-job`
  as the runtime account, 1 vCPU, 512 Mi, 300 s, one attempt, pool 2
  (`JOB_POOL_MAX_UNDERDECLARED:maintenance`). Its plain env is exactly the
  closed configuration plus `DEPLOYMENT_SOURCE_COMMIT`; it carries the OD-2
  bucket proof as the closed record the service renders (byte-equal); its
  secrets are `IDENTITY_LINK_SECRET` and, when a version is pinned,
  `DISTRIBUTION_GITHUB_API_TOKEN`, by reference at a pinned version. It never
  carries `HOST_MODE`, `K_SERVICE`, `PG_TEST_*`, a `POSTGRES_MAINTENANCE_JOB_*`
  tunable or `CLOUD_RUN_JOB`. **CR-3's own `maintenance-job` profile reader
  accepts the render**, and 23 doctored renders each fail the binding.
- **It waits for its inputs, with its trigger.** The telemetry namespace, the
  bucket proof and the required secret's pinned, enabled version
  (`TELEMETRY_STORAGE_NAMESPACE_UNASSIGNED`, `BUCKET_PROOF_UNPINNED`,
  `SECRET_VERSION_UNPINNED:<name>`, `SECRET_VERSION_UNAVAILABLE:<name>`); none is
  a clean deferral, and a live job whose secret becomes unavailable gets a
  deferred update, never a delete.
- **Staging waits for its template.** The staging profile reads origins and
  identity values the desired state does not carry; they arrive with STG-PREP's
  staging service template. Until then the staging job, its trigger and the
  scheduler grant are deferred (`STAGING_MAINTENANCE_JOB_ENVIRONMENT_UNAVAILABLE`,
  never clean) and the staging rollout target omits the job. The production
  rollout target now moves it with the other jobs.
- **Budget.** The scheduled jobs add 2 connections to the budget: 84 of 100
  (was 82).

## Review follow-up: the v1.2 manifest read is bounded

A review found that `v12_newest_ready_manifest_age_seconds`, read as
`SELECT max(ready_at) FROM telemetry_v12_day_manifests WHERE state = 'ready'`
every five minutes, was an unindexed scan of a table that only grows (ready
manifests are retained source, migration 0035). The first two commits proved the
journal reads walk a primary key but never the manifest read. Verified, and
fixed:

- **Confirmed.** Primary 0006 gives the table a primary key on `id`, a unique key
  `(participant_id, device_id, chunk_day, manifest_digest)` and two indexes that
  both lead with `participant_id`; no later migration indexes `ready_at` or
  `state`. The read was therefore a sequential scan whose only bound was the
  probe's 2000 ms statement timeout, and the signal would have drifted from a
  value to `unavailable STATEMENT_TIMEOUT` as the table grew.
- **The no-migration route does not exist.** An index ordered by `created_at` could
  have bounded a window, but both existing time indexes lead with `participant_id`
  and PostgreSQL 17 has no skip scan, so no existing index can answer "newest
  overall". A window by `created_at` would also be a different quantity: a manifest
  becomes ready after it is created. So the fix is an index.
- **Staged migration**
  `apps/worker/postgres/staged-migrations/primary/0921_v12_ready_manifest_ready_at_index.sql`:
  `CREATE INDEX telemetry_v12_manifests_ready_at ON telemetry_v12_day_manifests (ready_at) WHERE state = 'ready'`.
  Purely additive. The probe's SQL did not change. `0921` is a placeholder; the
  integrator numbers it at promotion. Cost, in the file's own header: `CREATE
  INDEX` takes a SHARE lock for the build (the runner's 5 s lock and 30 s
  statement timeouts bound it, and it rolls back whole), `CONCURRENTLY` cannot
  run in the runner's transaction, and a ready transition is no longer a heap-only
  update (one extra index entry per manifest).
- **Spec.** `ops-runtime-probe.spec.mjs` now builds its schema through the
  staged-migrations harness (the stock chain plus this file, found by its name
  suffix whether staged or promoted, so the integrator's rename needs no spec
  edit). A new test seeds 30,000 manifests (10,000 ready, 20,000 staged), captures
  the statement the probe really issues, and asserts through `EXPLAIN` that it is
  an `Index Only Scan` or `Index Scan` backward on `telemetry_v12_manifests_ready_at`
  with no sequential scan of the manifests, and through `EXPLAIN (ANALYZE)` that
  the scan returns one row and removes none. A control drops the index inside a
  transaction, shows the same statement is a `Seq Scan` even with sequential scans
  discouraged, rolls back, and checks the index definition is intact.
- **Until the index is promoted and applied** the probe is unchanged and still
  fails honestly (`unavailable STATEMENT_TIMEOUT`, never a wrong value). The probe
  is not registered, so nothing reads it. Registering the probe jobs now also
  requires promoting this migration (checklist item D-OPS4-FU).

## Not covered, and gates that remain

- **No live qualification.** Cloud SQL's `pg_stat_activity` and
  `pg_stat_database` behaviour, the Admin API's real response shapes (the OPS-1
  policy fails closed on any field it does not recognize, which would read as
  level 2), the Cloud SQL logs, Cloud Run Job and Cloud Scheduler behaviour, and
  the manifest's job render against `gcloud run jobs replace` are all
  unqualified until the first owner-run readback and apply (OPS2-READ).
- **The probe jobs are not registered, and the ready_at index is not promoted.**
  They are not in `JOB_NAMES` or the committed desired state, and the manifest check still pins `ops-runtime-probe`
  and `ops-backup-audit` as absent. Registering them needs an owner decision on
  the `opsBackupAudit` account (and its two permissions), the image roll, two
  triggers created paused, and the session-signal role below. Alerts on their
  lines wait for the monitoring bundle (E-OPS5) and the notification channel
  (OWN-5c). The K-DETECT job (`unseen-token-probe`, on O-OPS) is also not a
  manifest job: O-OPS's policies defer on `PRODUCER_NOT_IN_MANIFEST` until one
  is registered, which is a later change.
- **The session signals need a role decision (owner).** Either grant
  `pg_read_all_stats` to a role the probe uses, accepting that role can read
  other sessions' query text, or leave the three signals `PRIVILEGE_MISSING`. A
  narrower route (a security-definer function owned by a monitored role that
  returns only counts) needs a migration and was not built.
- **Nothing is applied or resumed.** The maintenance job and trigger exist in
  the committed desired state and the rendered plan only. The first apply, the
  resume (OPS-3) and the first scheduled pass are open. The first-roll ready
  path (a pass between `migrate` and `roll`) is still D-CRB's.
- **A maintenance-specific alert is not built.** `maintenance_completed_age_seconds`
  over 2 hours is the direct readiness signal; the scheduler-quiet proxy is not.
- **The `maxConnections` of each probe** is not in the budget (the runtime
  probe's one connection fits the 10 of headroom); registration adds it.
- **The 300 s task timeout** of the maintenance job is an engineering choice
  from the job's own bounds, not a measurement.
- **R7 receipts.** No root product code or workload source changed, so the two
  R7 tests are not expected to move; they were not run beyond `test:changed`.
